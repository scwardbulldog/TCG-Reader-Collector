# What Bandai's official lists can supply per Printing (Variant kind, rarity code, Release Product)

Research for [#57](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/57) (feeds [#55](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/55)), **Bandai source only**. Other sources (optcgapi, Limitless, onepiece.app, marketplaces) are covered by sibling notes. It extends, and does not redo, [printing-scenarios.md](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/blob/research/printing-scenarios/docs/research/printing-scenarios.md) (#52) and [catalog-sources.md](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/blob/research/catalog-sources/research/catalog-sources.md) (#2). Card data is Bandai's. This note quotes a few IDs only; every count was computed on 2026-11-27 from the private `TCG-Catalog` files (EN 5,119 Printings generated 17:46 UTC, JP 5,270 generated 17:15 UTC) or from live Bandai pages fetched the same day. Nothing was copied out of the private repo, and I did not modify it. Paths of the form `TCG-Catalog:pipeline/build.mjs:N` are the private repo; `vegapull@f8b5cfa` is the pinned fork (`scwardbulldog/vegapull`, rev from `TCG-Catalog:.github/workflows/catalog.yml:16-18`).

## Summary

1. Per card, Bandai's list shows about 17 fields: ID, a number span (= Base Number), rarity code, category, name, image, cost, attribute, power, counter, colour, block icon, type line, effect text, trigger, the "Information Obtained" product text, and link buttons. The pipeline keeps **6** values (`id`, `baseNumber`, `releaseProduct`, `rarity`, `name`, `imageUrl`). Rarity is published as a long enum name, not Bandai's code.
2. **Nothing in the card markup says parallel, manga, SP, foil or promo.** Bandai has exactly two server-side signals: the `_rN` suffix (its own "Reprinted Card" filter hides exactly the `_r` listings) and the rarity codes `SP CARD` and `TR`. Everything else with a `_pN` suffix is one undifferentiated "another Printing".
3. Bandai **does** carry two things the pipeline throws away: the **pack ID** (the `?series=` value, whose first digits encode the product type) and the **bracket product code** in the pack title (`[OP-01]`, unique per list). A third, a `Product Information` link slug, is in the HTML but not captured by the fork.
4. A hidden Bandai filter (`illustrations[]`: Comic / Animation / Other) exists in the search form but tags only about a quarter of Printings, and describes the art's origin, not a treatment such as manga.
5. Bandai's terms prohibit use beyond personal use without its prior consent; the card-list footer says the same of "all images, text and data". Card data must stay in the private catalog repo and published files, never in the public repo.

## 1. Fields per card

### What Bandai's HTML shows for one card

One `<dl class="modalCol" id="<card id>">` per listing. Observed on a live EN card (`OP05-119_p4`, PRB-01 page, 2026-11-27): `dt > div.infoCol` holds three spans (number, rarity, category), `div.cardName`, `dd > div.frontCol > img[data-src]`, then `backCol` blocks (cost, attribute icon, power, counter, color, block icon, `feature` type line, `text` effect, optional `trigger`), then `div.getInfo` with `<h3>Information Obtained</h3>` + free text + a button column.

The button column has a `CARDS` link (`./?search=true&series=<packId>`) and, for products with a page of their own, a `Product Information` link (`/products/boosters/<slug>.php`). Pages checked: coded packs (EN 569001, 569101, 569115, 569201, 569301; JP 550001, 550101) always carry it, with slugs like `prb01.php` or `op15-eb04.php`; starter decks share one (`/products/decks/st01-04.php` for the four decks of that group). The generic promo/other packs (EN 569801/569901, JP 550801/550901) carry only the `CARDS` link back to themselves. A few cards also have a second `div.getInfo.remarks` block ("Remarks", linking an errata notice).

JP pages add a per-card rulings Q&A block (`cardFaq*` classes). Neither list has an illustrator, copyright, illustration-type or finish field in the card markup.

### What the pipeline keeps

`TCG-Catalog:pipeline/build.mjs:39-46` builds each Printing from `id`, `baseNumber` (the ID minus `/_[pr]\d+$/`, line 6), `releaseProduct`, `rarity` (**the enum name**), `name`, `imageUrl`. Dropped: `pack_id` (except inside split `@packId` IDs, `:96-125`), `title_parts.label`, `category`, all gameplay fields, `info_obtained` for coded packs, the product link slug (never captured). Both Catalog files have exactly these six keys on every entry (checked: 5,119 EN, 5,270 JP).

### Rarity coding

The rarity span is a closed set of nine codes in both Languages; only the SP spelling differs (`vegapull@f8b5cfa` `config/en.toml:26-35`, `config/jp.toml:26-35`; the live search form lists the same values: `L C UC R SR SEC P "SP CARD" TR`).

| `UC` | Uncommon | 817 | 825 |
| `R` | Rare | 1,077 | 1,076 |
| `SR` | SuperRare | 719 | 760 |
| `SEC` | SecretRare | 144 | 153 |
| `L` | Leader | 344 | 367 |

(`rarity.rs:7-17,27-40`. Counts: private Catalog files.) Consequences: (a) the Catalog's "Special" is not Bandai's code, it is `SP CARD`; (b) the code `P` is not "is a promo": every `P-` number carries it (236 of 240 EN, 263 of 269 JP; the rest are Leader/Special/Rare), but promos of regular numbers keep their normal code, so 369 EN / 523 JP such Printings are not `P` (#52 counts the same effect); (c) the parser is an **exact-match closed enum** and a card whose rarity or category span does not match is **skipped with a log line** (`scraper.rs` `create_card` result handled at `src/scraper.rs:162-174`), see Defects.

### Product / pack identifiers vs free text

| Item | Form | Where | Pipeline keeps it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pack ID | numeric `series` value: EN `569xyy`, JP `550xyy` | `select#series > option[value]` | No (only in `@packId` split IDs) |

| Bracket code in pack title | `[OP-01]`, `[EB-04]`, `[PRB-01]`, `[ST-13]`, EN also `[OP14-EB04]`, `[OP15-EB04]` | `title_parts.label` | No (it survives only inside the free-text `releaseProduct`) |
| Pack title | free text, may contain `<br>`-stripped line breaks | `raw_title` | Yes, as `releaseProduct` for coded packs |
| "Information Obtained" | free text per card | `div.getInfo` | Only for the four generic promo packs (`build.mjs:10-13,36-37`) |
| Product page slug | `/products/boosters/prb01.php` | `getInfoBtn` link | No (never extracted) |

Measured on the live selects: 60 of 62 EN and 61 of 64 JP packs have a bracket code; none has two; **no code repeats within a list**; shapes are `OP-N`, `EB-N`, `PRB-N`, `ST-N` plus the EN-only `OPNN-EBNN`. The unlabelled packs are the generic ones (EN 569801/569901, JP 550801/550901) and the JP family deck set (550701). The code spelling is not consistent between the two Bandai fields: the pack title reads `[OP-01]` but the card's "Information Obtained" reads `-ROMANCE DAWN- [OP01]` (EN 569101, all 154 cards), so a code must come from the title, not from `info_obtained`.

For Printings in the generic promo packs the real product exists only as free text, 571 EN listings (375 + 196) and 733 JP (479 + 203 + 51 family deck; the Catalog holds 732 after merging one duplicate listing). The same bucket also holds product-exclusive cards that are not event prizes: EN `releaseProduct` values with their own bracket codes inside free text, `[GC-01]` Gift Collection (23 Printings in the Catalog) and `[SD-01]` Set Sail Deck Set (2), came through the generic packs. So "has a `[CODE]` in `releaseProduct`" is not a safe booster test; the pack ID is.

### `info_obtained`

Fork-only (`vegapull@f8b5cfa` `src/card/model.rs:37-39`, `src/card/scraper.rs:406-426`). On coded packs it repeats the pack title minus its type prefix (one distinct string per page in all five coded EN pages read). On the generic packs it is the real product: 31 distinct strings in EN 569801, 375 listings in 569901. Pipeline use: `build.mjs:36-37`; a blank value falls back to the pack title with a warning, and the build refuses to publish when more than half of promo Printings lack it (`build.mjs:60-70`).

### Image URL pattern

`https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/images/cardlist/card/<id>.png?<YYMMDD>` and the same path on `www.onepiece-cardgame.com`. All 5,119 EN and 5,270 JP `imageUrl`s follow it; the file name equals the Printing ID with any `@packId` removed (0 exceptions). Every URL in a file carries the **same** query value (one distinct value per file; e.g. `261124`), a site-wide cache-buster, so `imageUrl` changes in every entry whenever Bandai republishes assets, while the ID does not. The image URL says nothing about variant, and #52 already showed no two IDs share a URL.

## 2. Does anything in Bandai's data distinguish the kinds?

Counts are Printings in the Catalog files (EN / JP). "Coded product" means a booster, extra, premium or starter pack; "promo-ish" means the Printing sits in a generic promo pack or has a `P-` number.

| Kind asked about | Signal in Bandai data | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Reprint | `_rN` suffix (412 / 461). Bandai's own search flag `reprintsFlag=on` ("Hide reprint cards") removed **exactly** the `_r` listings on PRB-01 (112 of 319 hidden; the 206 `_p` and 1 plain ID stayed). `_r` appears only in PRB-01/02 and ST-15 to ST-36 packs (#52) and in 28 / 78 promo-ish Printings. | **Explicit.** A `_r` is Bandai's reprint. The reverse is not true: a card printed again with new art or foil is `_pN`, not `_rN` (#52 scenario 4). |
| SP | Rarity `SP CARD` (146 / 143 Printings, all with `_pN`; 3 / 3 in `P-` numbers). | **Explicit but only through the rarity code.** The ID does not say it. |
| Treasure Rare | Rarity `TR` (13 / 3, all `_pN`). | **Explicit, rarity code only.** |
| Parallel / alternate art | Only the `_pN` suffix (1,738 / 1,795 Printings; 622 / 655 bases have one `_p`, 285 / 285 have two, up to 9). 740 / 740 of them are in the same coded product as their plain Printing (the classic booster parallel); 505 / 496 are in a different coded product (an art reprinted or re-parallelled in a later product); 493 / 559 are promo-ish. Rarity matches the plain Printing in 1,579 of 1,738 (EN) and 1,645 of 1,795 (JP) where a plain Printing exists, so rarity does not mark a parallel either. | **Not distinguishable beyond "another Printing".** |
| Manga | Nothing. A manga Printing carries rarity `SEC` like other alt arts. In coded (base, product) groups with two or more `_p` Printings (142 in each Language), **124 groups** have two or more `_p` sharing a non-SP/TR rarity, covering 282 / 285 of 1,267 / 1,236 `_p` Printings: even if one of them is a manga, no field says which. | **Unknowable from Bandai.** |
| Foil / finish / stamp | Nothing. No finish field exists, and stamped promos can share an ID and image with the plain promo (#52 scenarios 8, 9, 10). | **Unknowable from Bandai.** |
| Promo | (a) `P-` number prefix (240 / 269); (b) the generic packs' bucket, which holds 571 / 733 listings including promos of regular numbers (369 / 523 Printings with regular numbers); (c) the free-text product. Not rarity (see above). | **Explicit as a bucket**, via pack ID; the sub-kind (regional, championship, magazine, box topper, gift set) is free text only. |
| Language-specific numbering | `_pN` is numbered per Language and has gaps (#52, ADR 0002). | Suffix number carries no meaning across Languages. |

Name carries no variant marker either: #52 measured that, inside a Language, the name is a function of the Base Number (0 of 1,144 multi-Printing EN bases differ). A scan for variant-ish words or parentheses finds 69 EN / 71 JP names, but they are character disambiguators spread across all suffix classes (EN: 47 plain, 16 `_p`, 6 `_r`), not Printing labels.

### A hidden Bandai signal: the illustration filter

Both search forms have an `illustrations[]` filter (EN labels Comic / Animation / Original Illustrations / Other; JP values 原作 / アニメ / オリジナル / その他). Probing it as a GET (`?search=true&illustrations%5B%5D=<value>`) returns different card sets, but:

| Class | EN listings | JP listings |
|---|---|---|
| Comic / 原作 | 804 | 865 |
| Animation / アニメ | 350 | 372 |
| Other / その他 | 38 | 80 |
| Original / オリジナル | 0 | 0 |
| **Classified of Catalog total** | **1,192 of 5,118** | **1,317 of 5,270** |

No card falls in two classes. The class is not shown in any card's HTML (the `Card.illustration` field is commented out in vegapull, `src/card/model.rs:21,48-54`), only reachable by filtering. Coverage is ~23% / ~25%, so most Printings have no tag, and "Comic" means *art taken from the manga*, which ordinary base cards also use (481 EN plain-ID Printings are Comic). It is a weak, partial art-source tag, not a manga-treatment flag. I probed it once per value (the first JP probe used English tokens and returned 0; re-run with the real JP values); Bandai may change or fill it.

### Defects found while reading the sources

These matter because a published structured field would inherit them.

1. **A Remarks block silently drops `info_obtained`.** `fetch_info_obtained` uses `get_child_node("dd div.getInfo")` (`src/card/scraper.rs:406-413`), which errors when the selector matches more than once (`:479-488`); the `Err` is swallowed into `None`. Cards with a Remarks block have a second `div.getInfo.remarks`, so they match twice. Live check: JP promo pack 550901 has one such card (`OP01-070_p2`) and it is the **one** JP Catalog entry whose `releaseProduct` is the generic title `プロモーションカード`. No EN generic-pack card currently has a Remarks block (569801 and 569901: 0), so EN is unaffected today. Any new Remarks on a promo would repeat this, and 75 of 154 EN OP-01 booster cards already have one (harmless there because coded packs do not use `info_obtained`).
2. **A new or mistyped rarity/category skips the card.** The localizer matches exact strings (`src/localizer.rs:43-65`); an unknown value fails `create_card`, which `fetch_cards` logs and skips (`src/scraper.rs:162-174`). Live check: JP `P-160` currently has the category text `キャラクタークター` (a typo on Bandai's page) and is **missing from the JP Catalog**. The other pages I diffed match the Catalog ID-for-ID. The publish gate only checks a >10% drop (`catalog.yml:63`), so single skipped cards are invisible.
3. **Bandai's own product text drifts**: `[OP01]` in `Information Obtained` against `[OP-01]` in the pack title, `OP14-EB04`/`OP15-EB04` as EN products where JP has `EB-04`, and trailing spaces and hyphen placement differ (`-Straw Hat Crew-[ST-01]`). Free text cannot be the product key.

## 4. Bandai's terms on ingesting and republishing

Primary texts, read 2026-11-27:

- **Bandai Site Terms of Use**, EN [bandai.co.jp/en/site/notice/](https://www.bandai.co.jp/en/site/notice/) and JP [bandai.co.jp/site/notice/](https://www.bandai.co.jp/site/notice/) ("Restrictions on Use" / 利用制限): all rights in "character designs, images, photographs, videos, music, audio, text, games, and programs (the 'Published Information')" belong to Bandai or its licensors, and "Any use of the Published Information (including but not limited to reproduction, modification, display, distribution, licensing, sale, and publication) beyond personal use or other use permitted by applicable laws and regulations is prohibited without the prior consent of Bandai." The JP text says the same (私的使用その他法令等によって認められる範囲を超えて ... バンダイの事前許諾がない限り、禁止). The "Disclaimer" adds that Bandai does not guarantee the accuracy of the Published Information.
- **The card-list pages' own footer**: "All images, text and data on this website may not be reproduced without permission." (EN cardlist page, footer line 9723 of the HTML fetched) and "このwebサイトに記載されているすべての画像・テキスト・データの無断転用、転載をお断りします。" (JP, line 10328). Both pages credit "© Eiichiro Oda/Shueisha, Toei Animation".
- **IP regulations**, JP [onepiece-cardgame.com/news/01_379.html](https://www.onepiece-cardgame.com/news/01_379.html) (2026-07-10, Bandai Card Division) and EN title [en.onepiece-cardgame.com/information/02_390.html](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/information/02_390.html): asks players not to sell or distribute cards or accessories made without permission from Bandai's published card designs or ONE PIECE characters, and states the regulations are neither a licence nor a waiver of legal rights. Not a data licence either way. (I read the JP text in full; the EN page body is script-rendered and I confirmed only its title.)
- **No robots.txt** on either site (both return 404), and the terms do not mention automated access. No developer or API terms exist for the card list. Bandai's cardlist scraping by vegapull therefore rests on its terms' "personal use" carve-out; the workflow identifies itself with a custom user agent and sleeps one second between packs (`TCG-Catalog:.github/workflows/catalog.yml:54,60`).

What follows for this project (a reading of the terms, not legal advice):

- The Catalog is a reproduction of Bandai's names, rarity text, product text and image URLs, and the pipeline runs twice a week (`catalog.yml:5`). Personal use is the carve-out; **any public redistribution of that data (including a public repo, release asset, or open file) is outside it** and needs Bandai's prior consent. That is why the data lives only in the private `TCG-Catalog` repo and is delivered to the app with a read-only token (`TCG-Catalog:README.md:3`, private; public format-only doc [catalog-format.md:3](../catalog-format.md)).
- Adding fields (rarity code, pack ID, product code, product kind) adds no new category of risk: they are all taken from Bandai's page, so they ride in the same private published file. A `variantKind` label is our own derived classification, not Bandai text, but it is derived from, and shipped with, Bandai data, so it travels under the same restriction.
- **Never in the public repo:** card names, IDs beyond a few illustrative examples, rarities per card, product titles per card, image URLs. Test fixtures in the public repo must be invented data. Counts and format documents are fine (as in this note). The note's own cards (`OP05-119_p4`, `OP01-070_p2`, `P-160`) are quoted only as IDs.
- Images stay by-reference (the Catalog stores Bandai's URL, not a copy), which is the lightest touch; the app must hotlink or cache locally for personal use only.
- Bandai's own text may change at any time ("Changes to the Terms of Use": revised without notice), and its "Published Information" disclaimer gives no accuracy guarantee, so the Catalog is a snapshot of an unwarranted source.

## Recommendation (Bandai-only part)

1. **Publish `rarityCode` as a structured field** from Bandai's code (nine values). It is the cleanest field Bandai offers; stop publishing the enum name.
2. **Publish `productId` (pack ID), `productKind` (from the pack-ID digits) and `productCode` (the bracket label)** next to the free-text `releaseProduct`. They replace the fragile "is it a promo?" tests (`[CODE]` presence, `P-` prefix, rarity) with Bandai's own bucket. `productCode` is optional (null for generic promo packs and the JP family deck set).
3. **Publish `variantKind` with an honest, small enum** (normal, reprint, sp, treasure-rare, promo, alternate-art) computed in the pipeline; document that alternate-art includes manga, foil and any other treatment. Do not claim manga, foil or stamp from Bandai data.
4. **Fix the pipeline defects before relying on any of this**: the Remarks double-match (defect 1), the silent skip of unknown rarity/category (defect 2). Both are visible today (one JP Printing with the generic title, one JP Printing missing).
5. The suffix stays in the opaque ID for identity (ADR 0002); a structured `variantKind` makes the app stop parsing it.
6. Anything finer (manga, foil, stamp) needs a second source or collector input; see the sibling notes.

## Not verified

- Counts of `reprintsFlag` and the illustration filter were probed once; the reprint test covered PRB-01 only, EN only.
- Cards skipped by the strict rarity/category parser were checked on 12 live pages (7 EN, 5 JP), not all 126 packs. Only one missing card (`P-160`) was found.
- Pack-ID stability across time was not measured; the private Catalog does not store pack IDs except in `@` IDs.
- I did not compare EN and JP pack-ID serials (e.g. whether the last two digits line up); only the type digits were examined.
- Whether a `_rN` is always the same illustration as its plain Printing is inferred from the data and Bandai's reprint flag, not checked against images.
- The Asia-English, Traditional Chinese and Simplified Chinese lists were not examined here.
- The EN IP-regulations page body was not readable (script-rendered); only the JP text was read in full.
