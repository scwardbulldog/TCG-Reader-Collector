# v1 spec: scan and inventory One Piece cards

Hand-off spec for a build effort. It restates every decision from the [wayfinder map](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/1) in full; the linked tickets remain the record of the reasoning. Vocabulary is defined in [GLOSSARY.md](../../GLOSSARY.md): **Game**, **Collection**, **Catalog**, **Printing**, **Printing identifier**, **Unresolved Printing**, **Base Number**, **Language**, **Variant**, **Alternate Art**, **Reprint**, **Release Product**, **Owned Copy**, **Location**, **Scan Session**.

Decision records: [native Android over PWA](../adr/0001-native-android-over-pwa.md), [Printing key](../adr/0002-printing-key-game-language-bandai-id.md), [phone-only storage](../adr/0003-phone-only-storage-no-backend.md).

---

## 1. Overview & goals

A personal phone app for one user (the repo owner, on a Pixel 10 Pro) that inventories One Piece Card Game cards by scanning them. The collector points the camera at one card at a time, the app proposes candidate **Printings**, and the collector taps to confirm. Each confirmed card becomes one **Owned Copy** on the phone. The collection can be exported to Google Sheets for viewing on a PC and backed up and restored.

Scope of v1:

- One **Game**: One Piece. The model must not preclude Dragon Ball Super and Gundam later (section 8).
- Two **Languages**: English and Japanese.
- Single user, starting from an empty **Collection**.
- Online-only is acceptable; anything hosted is free or near-free.

Out of scope is listed in section 9.

Source: [wayfinder map](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/1), [Which Chinese edition(s) does v1 cover?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/9).

**Acceptance criteria**

- The app runs on the collector's Pixel 10 Pro and lets them build a One Piece **Collection** of EN and JP **Owned Copies** from an empty start using scanning.
- No feature requires importing an existing inventory.
- No feature requires a Chinese **Printing**.

## 2. Platform & architecture

- **Native Android app:** Kotlin and Jetpack Compose, with CameraX for the camera and ML Kit text recognition for scanning. Not a PWA: the scanning prototype showed on-device ML Kit reads card numbers reliably on the Pixel 10 Pro, while the browser path (Tesseract.js) was impractical.
- **Distribution:** sideloaded APK. No Play Store.
- **Devices:** the collector's Pixel 10 Pro and recent Android versions only.
- **Portable core:** domain and data logic (**Printings**, **Owned Copies**, matching rules) lives in a module with no Android dependencies, and the scanner sits behind an interface. A later Kotlin Multiplatform / Compose Multiplatform port to iPhone is then a port, not a rewrite. iPhone support itself is not part of v1.
- **No PC access, no hosted backend:** the **Collection** lives only on the phone. The PC gets a Google Sheets export (section 7).

Source: [Native Android app or PWA?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/5), [Prototype card-number scanning on the Pixel 10 Pro](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/4), [How reliably can a phone camera identify a One Piece card's number?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/3).

**Acceptance criteria**

- The app is a Kotlin / Compose Android project using CameraX and ML Kit text recognition (bundled Latin model).
- The domain module has no dependency on Android classes, and the scanner is invoked through an interface, so the domain module's tests run on the JVM without a device.
- The app installs from a sideloaded APK on the Pixel 10 Pro.
- No part of the app depends on an account, a backend the collector runs, or a Play Store service.

## 3. Catalog

**Source of record.** The official Bandai card lists for English ([en.onepiece-cardgame.com/cardlist](https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/cardlist/)) and Japanese ([onepiece-cardgame.com/cardlist](https://www.onepiece-cardgame.com/cardlist/)). They cover cards from regular products, **Variants**, **Reprints**, and promos and not-for-sale cards (the "Promotion card" pack, event and tournament prize packs, and "other product" cards). The `punk-records` mirror is not used directly: its JP data was stale at the time of the decision. Third-party APIs (optcgapi, apitcg) are not used: they are English-only and hobby-hosted.

**Pipeline.**

- A separate **private** repo (for example `TCG-Catalog`) runs a daily GitHub Actions job with [`vegapull`](https://github.com/Coko7/vegapull) against the official sites.
- The job validates its output, for example by refusing to publish if the **Printing** count drops sharply, so a site-layout change cannot replace the last good **Catalog**.
- It publishes one compact **Catalog** file per **Language**, as `catalog/<game>-<language>.json` on `main`. The format (format only, no Bandai data) is in [catalog-format.md](../catalog-format.md); the gate refuses a drop of more than 10%.
- Parser breakage is fixed in the job, with no APK rebuild or reinstall.
- The repo is private because Bandai's terms allow personal use only: the public app repo never republishes Bandai's data. The app downloads the files with a fine-grained, read-only token scoped to that one repo (leak exposure is a card list).

**On the phone.**

- The app keeps the whole **Catalog** locally (about 5,000 **Printings** per **Language**) and matches scans and manual searches against it, with no network round-trip per scan.
- It checks for a newer **Catalog** on launch and offers an "update catalog" button.
- Images load from the official Bandai image URL the first time a **Printing** is shown and are then cached on the phone. There is no bulk image download and no re-hosting.
- The **Catalog** stores each **Printing**'s Bandai ID and **Base Number**.

Source: [Which catalog source does v1 use, and is it looked up live or copied locally?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/7), [Which catalog sources cover One Piece Printings in EN, JP, and CN?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/2).

**Acceptance criteria**

- The Catalog pipeline runs daily in a private repo, validates before publishing, and publishes one file per **Language** (EN, JP); a sharp drop in **Printing** count blocks publication.
- A fresh install downloads both **Catalog** files, and scanning and search work with the network off afterwards (images not yet cached excepted).
- The app checks for a newer **Catalog** on launch and has an "update catalog" button.
- A **Printing**'s image is fetched from the official URL on first view, then served from cache.
- The public repo and the APK contain no **Catalog** data and no Bandai images; the only credential in the app is the read-only token.
- The **Catalog** includes promo **Printings** (for example `P-001` and `OP01-006_p3`).

## 4. Data model

**Printing.**

- **Key: Game + Language + Bandai's card-list ID**, for example (One Piece, EN, `OP01-001_p1`). The ID is treated as opaque.
- Stored as details, not part of the key: **Base Number** (`OP01-001`), **Release Product**, rarity, name, image URL.
- **Language** is in the key because Variant suffixes are numbered per **Language** (EN `_p1` is not JP `_p1`). **Game** is in the key so other Games can be added without ID clashes.
- Evidence of stability: comparing English snapshots from March and November 2026, none of the 4,007 earlier IDs disappeared or changed **Release Product**; the list only grew.

**Variant.** Three kinds, derived from the ID suffix:

- **Normal:** no suffix.
- **Alternate Art:** suffix `_pN`. Covers parallel, manga, SP, promo-stamped and similar, with no finer label. Alternate Arts are told apart on screen by image, **Release Product** and rarity. A finer taxonomy can be added later without changing the key.
- **Reprint:** suffix `_rN`. Kept as a separate **Printing**; confirmation offers the Normal **Printing** first, with **Reprints** listed under it.
- **Variants are not matched across Languages by suffix.**

**Owned Copy.**

- One record per physical card, tied to exactly one **Printing** by its key.
- Fields: **Printing** reference (its **Printing identifier**), condition, **Location**, notes. There is **no quantity field**: how many of a **Printing** are owned is the count of its **Owned Copies**.
- **Condition** is one of: Near Mint, Lightly Played, Moderately Played, Heavily Played, Damaged. The default is Near Mint.
- **Location** is a collector-managed named list. A **Location** is created inline when starting a **Scan Session** or editing an **Owned Copy**, can be renamed (the rename applies to every **Owned Copy** in it), and can be deleted only when no **Owned Copy** is in it.
- A scan adds one **Owned Copy**. Bulk entry (for example "add 10 of these") creates that many separate **Owned Copies**.

**Printings that vanish.** If a **Catalog** refresh drops a **Printing** an **Owned Copy** points to, the app keeps the last-known details, keeps the **Owned Copy** attached, and flags it "No longer in the Catalog". Nothing is deleted silently and nothing is remapped.

**Catalog and Collection are stored separately**, so a **Catalog** refresh can never erase **Owned Copies** (section 7).

Source: [What uniquely identifies a Printing, and how are Variants and reprints modelled?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/8), [Which catalog source does v1 use, and is it looked up live or copied locally?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/7).

**Acceptance criteria**

- Two **Printings** with the same Bandai ID in different **Languages** (or **Games**) are distinct, and an **Owned Copy** references exactly one.
- **Variant** is derived from the ID suffix as above; **Base Number** is stored but never used as identity.
- There is no quantity field on **Owned Copy**; counts are computed by grouping.
- Condition accepts only the five listed values, and a new **Owned Copy** defaults to Near Mint.
- A **Location** can be created inline, renamed (updating all its **Owned Copies**), and deleted only when empty.
- After a **Catalog** refresh that removes a referenced **Printing**, the **Owned Copy** remains, shows its last-known details, and is flagged "No longer in the Catalog".
- Replacing or refreshing the **Catalog** does not change any **Owned Copy** record.

## 5. Scan Session flow

**Recognition.** The scanner reads the **Base Number** only (ASCII format such as `OP01-001`, found in the card's lower-right area, so ML Kit's Latin recognizer works for both **Languages**). A regex and a **Catalog** lookup filter out OCR noise. OCR cannot tell **Variants** apart, so the app proposes the **Printings** for that **Base Number** in the session's **Language**, and the collector confirms.

**Session setup.** A **Scan Session** is for one **Game** (One Piece in v1, chosen automatically) and one **Language** (EN or JP, chosen by the collector, never auto-detected). The collector also picks a **Location** and a default condition at the start. Each add inherits them; the collector can override condition for a single add without changing the session default. Changing **Game** or **Language** starts a new **Scan Session**. Manual search stays in the session's **Language**.

**Locking.** Lock on a **Base Number** by OCR votes: 3 reads of the same number with gaps allowed, not consecutive frames. The vote count of 3 is the starting value and may be tuned. After a verdict, ignore the just-recorded **Base Number** until a different number is seen or no number is read for about 1.5 seconds. The scanner analyses only the on-screen card outline region, which must match the visible outline.

**Confirmation.**

- A single matching **Printing** is preselected but still needs a tap to add.
- With several matches, the collector must select the exact **Printing** before confirming. Normal is shown first, **Reprints** grouped beneath it as separate selectable **Printings**, and **Alternate Arts** distinguished by artwork, **Release Product** and rarity.
- Successful confirmation adds an **Owned Copy** and returns to the camera.

**Duplicates.** Default is one copy. A bulk add applies the same selected **Printing**, **Location** and condition to all copies and creates one **Owned Copy** per physical card. To scan another copy of the same **Base Number** individually, clear the frame first.

**Glare and fallbacks.**

- While waiting for a read, offer a tilt hint, a manual torch toggle, and manual search / type-in by **Base Number** or name.
- When a read has no **Catalog** match, say so explicitly and offer manual search or skip.
- No match or skip creates no **Owned Copy**.

Source: [How should the v1 scan loop handle confirmation, duplicates, and glare?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/10), [Prototype card-number scanning on the Pixel 10 Pro](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/4), [How reliably can a phone camera identify a One Piece card's number?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/3). A throwaway interactive prototype (simulated reads) is on the [`prototype/scan-session-flow`](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/blob/prototype/scan-session-flow/docs/scan-prototype/scan-loop-prototype.html) branch.

**Acceptance criteria**

- Starting a **Scan Session** requires a **Language**, **Location** and default condition; EN and JP are both selectable and the **Language** is never inferred.
- A scan with one matching **Printing** preselects it and adds an **Owned Copy** only after the collector taps.
- With several matching **Printings**, nothing is added until the collector selects one; order is Normal first, **Reprints** beneath it.
- Locking uses 3 votes with gaps allowed (tunable); after a verdict the same **Base Number** is not re-locked until a different number appears or about 1.5 s with no read.
- A bulk add of N creates N **Owned Copies**, each with the same **Printing**, **Location** and condition, and no quantity value.
- A per-add condition override does not change the session default.
- With no **Catalog** match, the screen says so and offers manual search or skip; neither creates an **Owned Copy**.
- The tilt hint, torch toggle, and manual search / type-in by **Base Number** or name are available while waiting for a read, and manual search returns only **Printings** of the session's **Language**.
- Recognition uses only the card-outline region; the on-screen outline matches the analysed region.

## 6. Collection views

V1 has **one Collection list** grouped by **Printing**, with search, filters, and a **Printing** detail screen for maintaining individual **Owned Copies**.

- **Grouping and detail.** Group by **Printing** identity, so **Languages**, **Alternate Arts** and **Reprints** stay separate even when they share a **Base Number**. Each row shows a derived **Owned Copy** count. Tapping a row shows its individual **Owned Copies**; each can be edited (condition, **Location**, notes) or deleted after confirmation.
- **Search and order.** Case-insensitive partial search by **Base Number** or name; entries that only have a saved Printing identifier also match on it. Default order: **Base Number**, then **Language**, then Printing identifier. Entries without a **Base Number** go last.
- **Filters.** Search and filters combine with AND. **Release Product**, **Language** and **Variant** match the **Printing**. **Release Product** is the product associated with that specific **Printing**, not the **Base Number** prefix, so a **Reprint** appears under its own release. **Location** matches individual **Owned Copies**: with two copies in Binder 1 and one in Bulk box A, filtering to Binder 1 shows two in the row count and the detail; clearing the filter shows all three.
- **Catalog gaps.** Both kinds stay in the default list with counts, and with individual edit and delete available:
  - **No longer in the Catalog**: a refresh dropped the **Printing**. Last-known details are used for display, search and filters.
  - **Unresolved Printing**: a restore has only the saved identifier. Show that identifier and **Language** without inventing a name, **Release Product** or **Variant**. It can match **Language** and **Location** filters, but not a selected **Release Product** or **Variant**.
  - Never hide, delete or automatically remap **Owned Copies** because of a **Catalog** gap.
- **Navigation.** The app opens on the **Collection** list. It has an "Add" button that starts a **Scan Session**, and a menu for CSV export, backup, restore and **Catalog** update. There is no separate home screen in v1 (a **Game** picker arrives with a second **Game**, section 8).
- **Deferred past v1.** Batch **Location** moves and per-**Release Product** dashboards.

Source: [Which collection views and filters belong in v1?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/11#issuecomment-7172681776).

**Acceptance criteria**

- One list shows one row per **Printing** with a derived count; the same **Base Number** in EN and JP, or **Normal** and **Reprint**, gives separate rows.
- Search is case-insensitive and partial on **Base Number** or name, and ordering follows the default order above.
- Filters by **Release Product**, **Language**, **Variant** and **Location** combine with search by AND. The Binder 1 / Bulk box A example above holds.
- The detail screen edits condition, **Location** and notes, and deletes an **Owned Copy** only after confirmation.
- "No longer in the Catalog" and "Unresolved Printing" entries appear in the default list with counts, editable and deletable; Unresolved entries do not invent metadata and are excluded by **Release Product** and **Variant** filters.
- The app opens on the **Collection** list, with an Add button that starts a **Scan Session** and a menu reaching CSV export, backup, restore and **Catalog** update.
- There is no batch move and no dashboard in v1.

## 7. Storage, CSV export & JSON backup/restore

**On-device storage.** A local SQLite database via Room. The **Catalog** and the **Collection** are stored separately, so **Catalog** refreshes cannot erase **Owned Copies**. Domain logic stays independent of Android.

**Google Sheets export (CSV).**

- Manual only. The collector exports a CSV snapshot of the entire **Collection**, across all **Games** and **Languages**, via Android's save/share flow, then opens or imports it in Google Sheets. There is no Sheets API integration and no automatic update of an existing Sheet.
- One row per **Owned Copy**, so separate records for the same **Printing** stay separate. There is no quantity column.
- Columns: Game, Language, Base Number, Printing identifier, name, Variant, **Release Product**, condition, **Location**, notes. Unavailable **Catalog** details are left blank.
- The Printing identifier is Game + Language + Bandai ID, for example `onepiece / EN / OP01-001_p1`.

**JSON backup and restore.**

- A separate, versioned JSON backup, saved manually through Android's document picker to a collector-chosen location such as Google Drive. It preserves every **Owned Copy** record exactly (**Printing** reference, condition, **Location**, notes and all other values) and never combines records. The **Catalog** and image cache are excluded (re-downloadable).
- Reminders: show one when the **Collection** changes after a successful backup write, and after a restore until a fresh backup is saved. Record a successful backup only after the write succeeds. A successful write does not guarantee that a cloud provider has finished syncing, or that the file is off the phone.
- Restore validates the complete file before changing anything. Damaged or unsupported backups are rejected with an error. After explicit confirmation, restore replaces the entire **Collection** atomically; a failure leaves the existing **Collection** intact.
- If a **Printing** reference in the backup is absent from the current **Catalog**, the **Owned Copy** and reference are retained and visibly flagged **Unresolved Printing**; never silently discarded or remapped. A later **Catalog** update may resolve it.
- Restoring this app's own backup is not the out-of-scope import of an existing inventory.

Source: [How is the collection stored on the phone, and how is it exported to Google Sheets?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/6) (with its amendment), [What uniquely identifies a Printing, and how are Variants and reprints modelled?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/8).

**Acceptance criteria**

- **Collection** and **Catalog** live in separate tables; a **Catalog** refresh never changes an **Owned Copy**.
- CSV export is manual, covers all **Games** and **Languages**, has one row per **Owned Copy**, the listed columns and no quantity, and leaves missing **Catalog** details blank.
- Backup is a manual, versioned JSON file written via the document picker, preserving every **Owned Copy** record exactly, with no **Catalog** or images.
- Backup status is recorded only after a successful write; the reminders appear in both stated cases.
- Restoring a damaged or unsupported file shows an error and changes nothing; a valid file replaces the **Collection** only after confirmation, atomically, and a mid-restore failure leaves the old **Collection** intact.
- Restoring a backup whose **Printing** references are absent from the **Catalog** keeps those **Owned Copies** and shows them as **Unresolved Printing**.
- A backup taken, then restored on a fresh install, reproduces the same **Owned Copies**.

## 8. Multi-Game compatibility boundary

V1 supports only One Piece, but must not force a rewrite of **Printing** identity, the **Catalog** shape or **Owned Copy** references when another **Game** is added. The boundary:

- **Printing identity.** Game + Language + opaque source ID is the stable key in the **Catalog**, **Owned Copy** references, CSV exports and backups. A future **Game** must supply stable **Printing** IDs within each **Language**, or a durable mapping to them. A reference whose **Printing** disappears is kept and flagged, never silently remapped by **Base Number**. Two **Games** can reuse the same number or source ID without colliding.
- **Catalog and recognition.** A future **Game** must supply normalized **Printing** records and a way to find and confirm the exact **Printing**. **Catalog** lookups, OCR matching and manual search stay within the selected **Game** and **Language**. **Base Number** is a lookup aid, not identity. One Piece's number format, OCR matching rules, Bandai suffix meanings and the resulting **Variant** classification do not apply to other **Games** by default. A single candidate still requires confirmation.
- **Collection and Scan Session.** One personal inventory, with each **Game**'s **Collection** as a filtered view. An **Owned Copy** gets its **Game** through its **Printing**. In v1, Add uses One Piece automatically, with no **Game** picker. With several **Games**, Add from a **Game**'s **Collection** inherits that **Game** and Add from Home asks for it once; the scanner never infers the **Game**. **Game** and **Language** are fixed for a **Scan Session**.
- **Not in v1.** No support for another **Game**, no plugin system, and no selection of future **Catalog** sources.

Source: [What must the v1 spec preserve to support another Game later?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/14#issuecomment-7172867774).

**Acceptance criteria**

- Every **Printing** reference, in the database, CSV and backup, carries Game + Language + source ID, with **Game** stored explicitly even though only One Piece exists.
- One Piece-specific rules (number regex, suffix-to-**Variant** mapping) live in One Piece-specific code or data, not in the shared **Printing** or **Owned Copy** model.
- **Catalog** lookups, OCR matching and manual search take a **Game** and **Language** scope.
- No **Game** picker appears in v1 UI.

## 9. Out of scope

- Valuations and pricing (the model merely must not preclude them).
- Graded slabs.
- Multi-card capture (binder pages or spreads).
- Offline scanning (the first **Catalog** download and image caching need the network).
- Importing an existing inventory (the **Collection** starts empty).
- Chinese (Simplified and Traditional) **Printings**; the collector holds only about five Simplified Chinese cards. The research on the Simplified Chinese source is in [Which catalog sources cover One Piece Printings in EN, JP, and CN?](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/2).
- Dragon Ball Super and Gundam **Catalog** sourcing, and support for those **Games**.
- iPhone support (a later port).
- Play Store distribution.
- A hosted backend or live PC access.
- Batch **Location** moves and per-**Release Product** dashboards (deferred from section 6).

Source: [wayfinder map](https://github.com/scwardbulldog/TCG-Reader-Collector/issues/1).

**Acceptance criteria**

- None of the items above is built in v1.

## 10. Open risks

None of these blocks starting the build; each needs measuring or watching.

- **Glare on foils.** The prototype's main weakness is glare on **Alternate Art** and secret-rare foils. Tap-to-confirm and manual search absorb it; torch, tilt hint and auto-exposure tuning are build-time work to measure. The prototype's quantitative data was lost, so v1 has no hit-rate benchmark: measure one early on the collector's own cards.
- **Card-outline region.** The prototype's analysed region did not match its on-screen outline, and ML Kit read the whole frame. Cropping to the outline should also cut time per frame.
- **Catalog pipeline dependence.** The **Catalog** depends on `vegapull` and the official site layouts; a layout change breaks the job until it is fixed (validation prevents publishing a bad **Catalog**, and the app keeps the last good one). Bandai's personal-use terms are the reason the pipeline and its data stay private.
- **Read-only token in the APK.** It is sideloaded for one user and scoped to one private repo of card data, so leak exposure is low; it can be rotated by rebuilding.
- **Variant granularity.** Three **Variant** kinds are coarse; **Alternate Arts** rely on image, **Release Product** and rarity. A finer taxonomy can be added later without changing the key.
- **Backup is manual.** The phone is the only copy of the **Collection**; the reminders reduce but do not remove the risk of a lost phone with a stale backup.
- **Build-time details not decided here.** The file formats of the **Catalog** and backup. These are for the build effort, within the constraints above.

**Acceptance criteria**

- Early in the build, a scan hit-rate and time-to-lock measurement is made on the collector's own EN and JP cards, including foils, and recorded.
